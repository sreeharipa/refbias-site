Findings so far

Click through for the charts behind each one.
01
Referees differ in how many fouls they give
10 of 27 regular referees are clearly stricter or more lenient than average. That's about 4 fouls a match between the extremes, and it holds across seasons (r = 0.74).
Clear effect
01
Referee × team favouritism
11 of 306 referee–team pairs had p < 0.05, against 15 expected by chance. 0 survive a multiple-testing correction.
No effect found
02
Strict referees → more goals?
A stricter referee adds 0.46 fouls per team, but goals don't move: −0.039 goals per extra foul (95% CI −0.12 to +0.03).
No effect found
03
Cards make defenders cautious (team totals)
Inconclusive: how many cards a referee gives per foul isn't a stable trait (r = 0.13), so the design has no lever. Needs the minute-by-minute test.
Mixed / inconclusive
04
After a yellow, does a team concede more?
Built and tested on simulated data. Waiting for match events: 90/1140 matches downloaded.
Waiting for data
05
Are referees assigned at random?
Senior referees get the big games (p < 0.001), but strictness is balanced across match types (joint p = 0.65). The earlier results stand.
Mixed / inconclusive
06
The crowd sways the referee
With fans, home teams get 14% fewer yellows than away teams; behind closed doors the gap vanished (0.86 → 1.03). Home advantage in points fell by 0.38 a match.
Clear effect
07
Referees matter more in close games (H2)
No sign of it. One test hit p < 0.05, but it didn't repeat across seasons (r = +0.05).
No effect found
08
Neutral-referee prediction model
W/D/L log loss 1.000, vs 0.992 for the bookmakers and 1.080 for base rates. That's 91% of the market's edge. Anytime-scorer layer included.
Clear effect
09
Do referees favour specific players?
Not in yellow cards: 0 of 1,538 referee–player pairs stand out, and the same pairs don't repeat across seasons (r = −0.01). Fouls won per take-on comes next.
No effect found

Where this is heading

The product shows, for every upcoming match, a neutral-referee prediction (built: see next gameweek) plus the shift caused by the assigned referee. On current evidence, that shift is in fouls and cards, not in results. Penalties, red cards and VAR decisions come next, from the match events backfill.
